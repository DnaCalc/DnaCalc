## E1. B3 band of GAMMALN: windows across [2, 3] (answer B.3)

Inputs: 258 windows W(c) of 1,024 consecutive doubles (bit patterns c-512 .. c+511), with centres
c = 2 + (i + 0.5)/256 for i = 0..255, plus 2.5 and 2.75: 264,192 inputs. Script `e1_windows.py`;
predictions file `e1-predictions.tsv`, SHA-256
`1711a679fce237907ff4042271798d0635896bd4a1e8296b4406f5cf959c3f05`.

Graphs (the shape question). All three evaluate `y = (x-2) * P(x)/Q(x)`, P and Q of degree 8 in x
itself, Horner from the top coefficient, each product and each sum stored to binary64:

- G0 (the landed graph): each polynomial's last sum stays in the register; `R = st(P/Q)`;
  `y = st(R * (x-2))`.
- G1: the last sums are stored too.
- G2: as G0, but R stays in the register for the final multiply.

Table H0 is the landed one (`gammaln.rs` `B3_P`, `B3_Q`). The predictions file holds H0 under G0, G1
and G2 for every input.

Predictions, frozen before asking:

1. Data check, not a test: H0 under G0 matches about 98% of inputs within 0.002 of 2.5 and about a
   third elsewhere on [2, 3] (the figures in the `b3_with` doc comment), and gives 3fd2383e809a67e9 at
   2.5 and 3fde69eac73eab08 at 2.75 (Excel: ...e8 and ...04, from the bank).
2. Shape: under G0 there is a table of 18 binary64 coefficients that matches Excel on at least 99.5% of
   the E1 inputs, and on every one of the 258 windows at least 97%. The two constant terms may move
   far from H0 (they were not read from stage lines); the other 16 move by at most 64 ulps each.
   Falsified if the best G0 table found stays under 99% overall.
3. Graph: at most one of G0, G1, G2 reaches 99.5% with its own best table. G1 and G2 change a rounding
   at every input; if Excel's graph is G0, a G1 or G2 table cannot follow its rounding phases.
4. Acceptance: the fitted G0 table gives Excel's GAMMALN(2.5) = 3fd2383e809a67e8 and GAMMALN(2.75) =
   3fde69eac73eab04.

Fitting is allowed here because the shape is fixed in advance: a coefficient table is a set of
constants inside G0. The search moves coefficients by whole ulps and scores the count of exact
matches; the result is then tested on inputs it never saw (E1b).

E1b (held out; its predictions are frozen with the fitted table, after E1 and before E1b is asked):
random inputs on [2, 3], the equal-spacing grid x = 2 + j 2^-51 (j = -512..511, j != 0) across 2, and
windows at 3 and 1.625.
